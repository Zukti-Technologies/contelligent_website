You open the bank ledger in Tally and it says ₹18.4 lakh. The bank statement says ₹21.1 lakh. Both are correct, and the gap is usually one of a handful of things.

## 1. Cheques issued but not yet presented

You record a payment in Tally the day you hand over the cheque. The bank only deducts it when the supplier deposits it, which can be days or weeks later. Until then, your books show less money than the bank does.

## 2. Deposits recorded but not yet credited

The reverse case. A customer's cheque is entered in Tally on the day you receive it, but clearing takes time.

## 3. Bank charges and interest nobody entered

Charges, GST on charges, interest credits and auto-debits for loan EMIs appear on the statement first. If your accountant enters them only at month end, the books lag behind.

## 4. Entries posted to the wrong bank ledger

With three or four bank accounts, a receipt meant for one account sometimes lands in another. Both ledgers are now wrong, in opposite directions.

## 5. Date differences

An entry dated 31 March in Tally may clear on 2 April. The balance at year end differs even though nothing is missing.

## Finding the cause

TallyPrime's bank reconciliation screen lets you mark each entry with the date it cleared the bank. Whatever remains unmarked is your list of differences. Do it every week rather than once a quarter, and the list stays short enough to check in ten minutes.

In Caliper, the Liquidity Tracker reads the same bank ledgers from Tally, so once your reconciliation is up to date, the cash position you see there matches the bank.
