Most cash problems in an MSME don't start with a loss. They start with customers paying a little later each month.

## The calculation

Receivable days = trade receivables ÷ annual revenue × 365.

Say your sundry debtors stand at ₹45 lakh and sales for the year are ₹3 crore. That's 45 ÷ 300 × 365, or about 55 days. On average, you wait 55 days to get paid.

If you give 30-day credit terms, 55 days means customers are, on average, close to a month late.

## Watch the trend, not the number

A single figure is less useful than the direction. If receivable days went from 42 to 48 to 55 over three quarters while sales stayed flat, more of your money is sitting with customers every quarter. You are funding them, often from your overdraft.

## What to check next

1. **Which customers.** Sort debtors by amount overdue, not by total balance. A few accounts usually explain most of the increase.
2. **Which bills.** Look at the oldest unpaid invoices for those customers. Disputed bills tend to sit quietly until someone asks.
3. **Whether terms changed.** A sales team offering longer credit to close deals shows up here months later.
